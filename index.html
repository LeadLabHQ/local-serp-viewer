How it works

When you search Google, it quietly pins the search to where it thinks you are — which is why the plumber you see at the top of your results is rarely the plumber your customer sees at the top of theirs. Google provides a location parameter (called uule, the same one Google Ads uses to target ads to a place) that sets that pin to a named town instead.

This tool does three things: it resolves the place you typed to a town and state using OpenStreetMap data, it writes that town into Google's location parameter in Google's own format, and it hands you the finished Google link. The search itself runs on Google, in your browser — we never see, fetch, or store the results.

That also means this is a viewer, not a measurement. It sets the town; Google still weighs the exact spot inside the town, the time of day, and — if you're signed in — your own history. For the cleanest read, open the link in a private window, and treat what you see as "what a customer in this town is looking at," never as a ranking report.

Questions owners ask

What does the Local SERP Viewer do?

It builds a Google search link with the search location set to the town you enter — using Google's own location parameter (called uule, the same one Google Ads uses to target places) — and opens it on Google in a new tab. You see the results page as a customer searching from that town sees it, instead of the results for wherever you happen to be sitting.

Is this my ranking?

No. This is a viewer, not a rank tracker and not a measurement. It sets the search location to the town; Google still weighs the exact spot, the time, and — if you are signed in — your history. Two checks an hour apart can differ. Use it to see what a customer in that town is looking at, not as a position report.

Why do the results differ from the same search on my phone?

Your phone searches from where you physically are, and Google localizes hard — a search from across town can return a different list. This tool sets the location to the town you name, so you see that town's version. If you are signed in to Google, your own history can still shade what you see; for the cleanest view, open the link in a private window.

Is anything I enter sent or stored?

Your search words are never sent by us — they go to Google only when you click the link, like any Google search. The place name you type is sent to OpenStreetMap's free Nominatim service to resolve it to a town and state; that one lookup cannot be done locally. We store nothing, and the only thing we count is an anonymous tally that the tool was opened and used — no values, no cookies, nothing that identifies you.

How do I know Google used the location I picked?

Scroll to the bottom of Google's results page. Google prints the location it used for the search there — it should name your town. If it names somewhere else, Google mapped the town to a nearby place in its own directory; try the town name with the state instead of a ZIP, since ZIP areas often span several towns.
